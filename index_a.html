Oracle · 2021–Present

Agentic AI Workspaces for Manufacturing

A broad suite of GenAI features, copilots, and autonomous agents for production, quality, and engineering workflows — deployed across 10+ regulated factory sites.

+
Agentic AIGenAICopilotsLLM EvalsPrompt EngineeringFine-tuningRAGHuman-in-the-loop
10+Sites adopted
8Agents in production
↓Hallucinations
↑Model correctness

Problem: Production managers, engineers, and quality analysts in regulated factories were buried in manual, repetitive workflows with no AI assistance — while the industry demanded AI-native experiences embedded directly in MES.

Three tiers of AI capability delivered:

  • Generative AI features — shift notes auto-generation and summary handoffs that synthesize production events, exception logs, and KPIs into structured narratives for outgoing supervisors
  • Copilots — Procedure Advisor (answers operator questions grounded in work instructions and SOPs via RAG) and Authoring Assistant (helps engineers draft, revise, and standardize work instructions using LLM with domain-specific prompting)
  • Autonomous agents — Disposition Assistant (recommends accept/reject/rework on non-conforming material), Resource Change Assistant (reallocates operators and equipment to unplanned events), Work Order Change Assistant (adjusts schedules, quantities, and routing dynamically), and Operations Assignment Assistant (matches operators to work orders by skill and qualification)

Model quality & AI engineering:

  • Built evaluation frameworks (LLM-as-judge) measuring correctness, conciseness, and tone — calibrated to manufacturing domain expectations and regulatory standards
  • Led prompt engineering to reduce over-reliance on guardrail rejections — rewriting prompts to steer models toward compliant responses without hard blocks, improving UX while maintaining safety
  • Drove fine-tuning on domain-specific datasets (work instructions, quality dispositions, change orders) to improve model fit for manufacturing terminology, structured outputs, and shop floor tone
  • Partnered with applied science on RAG pipeline tuning — retrieval quality scoring, chunking strategy, and grounding accuracy improvements
  • Defined human-in-the-loop checkpoints for high-stakes decisions and guardrails meeting GxP and 21 CFR Part 11 requirements

Operational Improvement via Value Stream Mapping

End-to-end value stream analysis of generator manufacturing lines — eliminating waste, rebalancing flow, and delivering measurable efficiency and space gains.

+
Six Sigma BBValue Stream MappingLeanKaizenKanbanMin-Max
50%Efficiency lift
20,000 ft²Floor space reclaimed

Problem: Generator assembly lines suffered from unbalanced workstations, excessive WIP, and inefficient material flow. Throughput was constrained and floor space was consumed by buffers that masked real bottlenecks.

What I did:

  • Led a cross-functional Kaizen team through a full DMAIC cycle using value stream mapping to document current-state flows across the generator assembly line
  • Identified seven major waste categories and quantified their impact on throughput and lead time
  • Redesigned future-state flow: rebalanced workstations to takt time, introduced Kanban pull signals, and implemented Min-Max stocking strategy
  • Optimized physical layout to eliminate material travel distance, reclaiming ~20,000 sq ft repurposed for a new product line
  • Achieved 50% improvement in production efficiency, validated through before/after time studies over 90 days post-implementation
Cummins · 2017 · Six Sigma Black Belt

Portfolio Optimization via Square Root Costing

Applied square root costing with Finance to quantify the true cost of product complexity — driving option rationalization and delivering hard savings in year one.

+
Six Sigma BBSquare Root CostingPortfolio OptimizationComplexity ReductionDMAIC
$800KYear-1 savings
↓SKU & option count
↑Schedule attainment

Problem: The power systems portfolio had accumulated significant complexity over years of customer-driven customization. The true cost of supporting low-volume options was invisible in standard costing models, making rationalization politically difficult and financially unsupported.

What I did:

  • Applied square root costing methodology in partnership with Finance to build a rigorous model quantifying the fully-loaded cost of each product option — inventory carrying cost, planner time, changeover impact, and supply base complexity
  • Ran a DMAIC project to baseline the portfolio, segment options by revenue vs. complexity cost, and identify rationalization candidates
  • Facilitated cross-functional alignment workshops with Product Management, Engineering, Sales, and Planning to build consensus
  • Developed a governance framework to evaluate future option requests against a complexity cost threshold — preventing re-accumulation
  • Delivered ~$800K in first-year savings; model adopted as standard practice for future portfolio reviews
