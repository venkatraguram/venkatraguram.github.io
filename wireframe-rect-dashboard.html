Top Insights & Recommended Actions
AI-synthesized from sample data · Prioritized by RECT impact
📚
Parts Catalog Quality — #1 Root Cause
34% of OOS events traced to incorrect part numbers in OEM catalogs. Dealers order wrong part, wait for delivery, discover error, restart order — adding avg 7.8 days per occurrence.
→ OEMs to audit and update catalogs quarterly. Implement part number validation at order entry.
−7.8d
⚖️
Warranty Authorization Bottleneck
Warranty repairs average 6.1 days waiting for OEM authorization — vs 1.8 days for regular repairs. Range spans 2–18 days across OEMs. No standard SLA exists. Completed repairs sit idle waiting for approval to close.
→ Establish 48-hr OEM warranty authorization SLA. Pre-authorize common repair categories.
−4.3d
🔧
Technician Training Gaps
Repairs performed by uncertified techs take 1.8× longer than certified — and have 2.4× higher re-work rates. New model launches see the sharpest spike as dealers lack training on new systems and components.
→ Mandate OEM training prior to new model retail launch. Fund dealer certification programs.
−3.7d
📅
Seasonal Capacity Planning Failure
RECT spikes 72% in Q2–Q3 peak season (18.1d → 31.2d). Dealers do not pre-position parts inventory or staff for predictable seasonal demand. Parts are ordered reactively when units arrive — avoidable delays.
→ Develop seasonal stocking guides by model mix. Incentivize pre-season tech certification completion.
−3.1d
🚐
Model Complexity Variance
Class A motorhomes average 2.3 days longer RECT than travel trailers — driven by system complexity (chassis, slideouts, electrical). Dealers with high Class A mix but low Class A certification rates show worst outcomes.
→ Route complex repairs to certified dealers. OEMs to publish complexity-tiered repair guides.
−2.3d
